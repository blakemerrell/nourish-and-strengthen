Privacy & your data

Last updated 7 October 2026

The short version. Hive & Hearth works entirely on your own device and needs no account. Nothing you enter leaves your phone unless you choose to sync it, or look a food up: the words of a food search go to the USDA’s food database, and a scanned barcode to Open Food Facts and the USDA, which are asked together so their answers can be checked against each other. There are no analytics, no advertising, no trackers, and nothing is sold or shared with anybody.

If you do sync, your weigh-ins, food log and workouts are readable only by your own sign-in, and you can delete the whole lot from inside the app at any time.

What is kept on your device

Everything the app does — browsing recipes, planning a week, building a shopping list, tracking your day in Nourish, logging a workout in Strengthen — is stored in your browser on your own device. That includes any weigh-ins, food you log, workouts you log, your plan, your favourites, your own recipes and your pantry.

If you never turn on syncing, that is the end of it. None of it is sent anywhere, and clearing your browser data removes it. The one exception is looking a food up, below.

What leaves your device, and when

Two separate things can be synced, and they work differently.

A shared household, joined by a code

If you join a household by typing a code, the plan the household shares is stored online so a second phone can see it. It holds:

  • Favourited recipes
  • Meal plans and which week is active
  • Recipes you have written yourself, and edits to existing recipes
  • What is in your pantry
  • Who is in it: the account identifier of each person who joined while signed in (a string of letters and numbers, not a name or an email)
  • Only if you turn it on in the sync panel: a dinner’s calories and protein from your Nourish plan, under a name you choose (it starts as the first word of your account’s name), so the people you cook with can plan dinners that fit it too — never your weight, your food log or the rest of your plan. Only your own sign-in can put them there or take them away, and turning the switch off takes them away

If you invite somebody with a link, the invite is stored as well: which household it opens, the account that made it, when it runs out, and the account that used it. A used or expired invite can no longer be read by anybody, but it is not deleted.

Anyone who has the code can read and change it. That is what the code is for. Treat it like a key: give it to the people you cook with and nobody else. A household is not tied to an account, and it holds nothing about your body or what you eat.

Your own record, if you sign in

Nourish and Strengthen are not shared, except those two dinner numbers if you choose to share them. If you sign in — with Google, or by an email link — so that your own phone and tablet stay in step, a record is stored against your sign-in containing:

Your workouts are kept in a record of their own for each calendar year, stored under your sign-in beside the record above and protected by the same rule: only your sign-in can read or change them. That is so years of training never fill one record.

Only your sign-in can read it. The rule on the database is that the record's owner and nobody else may fetch it; no other signed-in person, however they ask, gets an answer. It cannot be listed, searched or browsed by anyone.

Some of this is health information — your body weight and what you eat. It is treated as the private half of the app for exactly that reason, and it is never put in a household.

Signing in anonymously

To make the sharing above possible at all, the app signs you in anonymously the first time you join a household or sign in — not before: a visitor who does neither is never in touch with Firebase at all. This is an identifier with no name, no email and no personal detail attached, and it exists so that the database can tell one device from another. It is not an account, you are not asked for anything, and on its own it stores nothing about you.

Signing in with Google or an email link turns that anonymous identifier into one you can reach from a second device. At that point the app knows the email address you signed in with, so it can show you which account you are using.

Who processes it

Syncing and sign-in use Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication). Data synced by the app is stored on Google's servers under Google's terms. Nothing synced is shared with anyone else, and there is no analytics service, no advertising network, no crash reporter and no embedded tracker.

Two food lookups go to other services, and only when you use them. A food search in Nourish sends the words you typed to FoodData Central, the USDA’s food database, asked twice: once of its plain foods and once of its packaged foods, so a store brand can be found by name. A barcode you scan or type is looked up at Open Food Facts and, at the same time, in FoodData Central’s packaged foods, so that the two answers can be checked against each other — unless you have already saved a food from that barcode, in which case your own food opens and nothing is sent. When Open Food Facts has a photo of the packet, the app loads it from their image server to show you. Each receives that search or that number, the way any website receives a request (your internet address with it), and nothing from your log, your plan or your account; each handles it under its own terms.

Four links go elsewhere only if you tap them. Something wrong? Fix it there, under a scanned food, opens that product’s page on Open Food Facts; the barcode is all it sends. Find a video on YouTube, on a lift’s page, opens a YouTube search for that lift’s name in a new tab; the name of the lift is all it sends. The Walmart buttons on the shopping list open Walmart with the items you chose to buy there. What any of these sites does from there is under its own terms.

Or buy a bound copy opens a checkout run by Stripe. Stripe takes the payment, under its own terms, and passes us what you give it there that we need to send the book; nothing from the app goes with it.

How long it is kept

  • Weigh-ins and the food log — kept, every day of them, on your phone and in your account, until you delete them or the account. (Until October 2026 the phone let go of food days after 120 days and weigh-ins after 399; your account kept them, and a phone signed in to it now takes them back.) In the account they are kept one record per calendar year under yours, as your workouts are.
  • Your profile, plan and your own foods — kept until you change or delete them.
  • Workouts and training blocks — kept until you delete them. A workout in progress stays on the phone you are logging it on and is not synced until you save it.
  • A household — kept until somebody asks for it to be removed (see below). Meal plans for weeks more than about six months past are removed by the app on its own.

Deleting your account and everything in it

You can do this yourself, from inside the app, at any time:

  1. Open the app and tap SYNC at the top of the screen.
  2. Sign in, if you are not already signed in — the delete option only appears for the account it belongs to.
  3. Open Delete my account at the foot of that panel.
  4. Confirm. Your synced record and each year’s record of workouts and of your food log are removed, your sign-in is removed, and this device's copy is wiped.

Firebase asks for a recent sign-in before it will remove an account. If you are told to, sign out and back in, then delete — the app will say so rather than pretending it worked.

What deleting does not remove

A shared household is not yours alone, so closing your account does not delete it — taking a spouse's meal plan away would be a surprise nobody asked for. To leave a household, disconnect from it in the same panel. If you want a household removed entirely, write to us using the address below.

If you cannot get into the app

Write to blakemerrell@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, and ask for your record to be deleted.

Children

This app is not directed at children and asks for nothing from them. The recipe half needs no account at all and stores nothing about anybody.

Your choices, in one place

  • Use it with no account. The whole app works this way, and nothing is stored online.
  • Leave a household. Disconnect in the sync panel; your own copy stays on your device.
  • Sign out. Stops this device syncing, clears Nourish and Strengthen from it, and takes any dinner numbers you shared out of the household. Your account keeps everything it has already received.
  • Delete your account. Removes the record and the sign-in, any dinner numbers you shared with a household, and wipes this device. This cannot be undone.

On a phone or computer you share, also clear this site’s data in the browser after signing out or deleting. Firebase keeps its own offline copy of what it last fetched, and signing out does not yet remove that copy.

Changes

If what the app stores changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. There is no mailing list to notify, because we do not have your address unless you have written to us.
