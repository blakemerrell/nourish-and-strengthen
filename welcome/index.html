What it does
1

Make delicious food from the staples you keep

Every recipe here is built on about a hundred staples that keep: the canned, dry and frozen food a bishops’ storehouse, a food bank or a monthly shop hands you. Sunday roasts, meatballs, brownies, hot cocoa — not a short list of what you can get away with. Pick your meals for the week and they stay picked; the shopping list builds itself, and three recipes calling for cottage cheese come out as one line: 2⅛ cups. Scale any recipe up to eight times.

2

Know what to order, and what you still have to buy

Every recipe says at the foot of it whether your staples cover the whole thing, or names exactly what it does not — before you start, not halfway through. Filter the whole collection down to what needs nothing else at all. And the week’s shopping list comes out in two parts: what comes from your staples, and the short separate list for one trip to the grocery store.

3

Get an idea of how healthy a meal is

A score out of 100 on every recipe, from protein, calories, fat, sodium, fiber and how much of it is refined carbohydrate. A green leaf means cook it often; grey means know what you’re getting. Tap the leaf and it shows its working, part by part. It is worked out from a food table rather than off a label, so treat it as a good guess and not a lab result — but it is the same guess for all 365, which is what lets it sort them. Write your own recipe and it gets scored the same way.

4

It flexes with where your staples come from

A storehouse order, a food bank box and a monthly shop are not the same, and they do not stay the same. When yours stops carrying something, take it off the shelf here and every recipe in both volumes updates what it says you’d need to buy. The same switch is what keeps this useful if you stop going altogether.

5

Print it, use it, share it, add to it

Print the lot for yourself — half-letter, contents page, real typesetting, and no recipe split across a page break. The PDFs are already made, so you send one to a printer and that’s it. Or keep it on your phone, where one code puts the whole household on the same week and the same list. Write your own recipes in and correct any of the printed ones; both print alongside the rest in the same hand.

Other ways to print it

Both volumes are also imposed for folding, so you can print double-sided, fold down the middle and staple: Run and Not Be Weary (150 recipes) and Around the Table (215). Those two files are deliberately out of page order, so don’t hand one to a copy shop for spiral binding — give them the full book above.

There is a two-volume edition as well, if you would rather have them as separate books than as one.

Handing these out? There’s a one-sheet handout — eight storehouse-only recipes over both sides, with the code on each, made to be photocopied and sent home with an order (PDF).

Where this came from

My wife and I have needed the bishops’ storehouse a couple of times now.

Once in 2017, when I was on the wrong side of a RIF. And again now, with what used to be a good salary and no way to make ends meet on one income. We should be able to make it. But here we are.

Part of how we got here: we moved back to Utah in 2020 to be near family, and it looked right. Then it wasn’t. Infertility kept us at one child, we spent tens of thousands on doctors, and we lost a pregnancy, which about destroyed us. So we decided to be content with what we had and build our house — and once we had the permits, our second decided to be born. A house and a baby at the same time, and getting my startup AS9100 certified through all of it. Then the build came back at over half a million, so I built it myself. Years in the roof truss industry; nail guns don’t scare me.

And my family showed up. One brother-in-law brought his excavation equipment, another works at his family’s lumber and hardware store. My brother came weekend after weekend, setting roof and laying sheathing. My dad came nearly every day he could and did the rough electrical for the whole house. The whole house. It blows me away.

So when I went to my bishop, it was after doing everything I knew how to do.

A few months into the storehouse order, I was looking at all that food and wondering what I could make from it that my kids would love. This is what came of it.

It’s helpful to us, and I hope it’s helpful to you. We love the Lord, and we are glad to put this in the hands of anybody it might help.
